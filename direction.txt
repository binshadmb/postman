Analyzed cost margins between India and European pricing structures
Analyzed cost margins between India and European pricing structures
Short answer: yes — by a wide margin.

Example: 1-page A4 B&W letter, Speed Post

Item	European reference price (what sender is used to paying)	Your actual India-side cost
Print	~€0.20–0.30 (~₹18–28)	~₹2–5 (India print/photocopy rate)
Post	€0.95–1.80 (~₹85–165)	₹18–25 (Speed Post up to 50g)
Total	~₹105–195	~₹20–30
If you charge anywhere near the European reference (say ₹100–150 all-in), your actual cost is ₹20–30. That's 70–85%+ gross margin on a basic single-page letter, before your service/platform fee even factors in.

Why the gap exists:

India print costs are a fraction of Europe's (labor + paper cost differential)
India Post inland rates are famously cheap vs Europe's national postal operators
The sender never sees or compares India-side costs — they're anchoring to their own local postage/print experience, which is what makes this pricing psychologically fine for them and highly profitable for you
Caveats to keep in mind:

Heavier/multi-page docs, color, A3, registered mail, courier (vs post) all shrink the margin somewhat — but still stay well above India-side cost
Currency conversion (EUR/USD → INR) fluctuates — build a buffer, don't peg exactly to spot rate
Payment gateway fee (Razorpay ~2%), platform costs, and your labor/overhead eat into the margin — factor those in before calling it pure profit
Don't literally charge "the same €/£ number converted to ₹" for everything — for heavier categories (A3 bulk, courier, international ad placement) you may still want to build the price up from actual cost + margin, not just anchor to Europe, or it could look overpriced for premium/registered items where local competitors (courier apps) may quote lower
